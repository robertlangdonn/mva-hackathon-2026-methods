# Proposed experimental analysis plan

No experiments have been conducted for this proposal. This is a design for
discussion with a qualified laboratory, not an executable protocol or treatment
recommendation. Reference numbers refer to the accompanying Track 2 report.

## Primary question and model validity

Does nicotinic acid reduce chromosome-segregation errors in a BUBR1-deficient
model while preserving completed divisions and viable-cell output?

Use an authenticated disease-relevant model and a suitable WT reference. Where
possible use matched genetic backgrounds and more than one independent model.
An isolated construct tests a protein hypothesis, not the entire patient's
biallelic background. Demonstrate a reproducible defect and WT-restoration response
before screening the candidate. A failed restoration control makes the assay
uninterpretable rather than proving the drug ineffective.

## Minimum comparison set

| Comparison | Question answered |
|---|---|
| WT vehicle versus disease vehicle | Is the baseline phenotype measurable? |
| Disease with WT BUBR1 restoration versus disease control | Can the chosen phenotype respond to functional BUBR1? |
| Disease plus nicotinic acid versus disease vehicle | Is there a candidate response? |
| WT plus nicotinic acid versus WT vehicle | Does the intervention impair normal division/output? |
| Candidate with and without controlled SIRT2 perturbation | Is the response consistent with the proposed SIRT2 bridge? |
| Appropriately controlled BUBR1 perturbation/restoration | Is functional benefit attributable to BUBR1 rather than other SIRT2 targets? |

Include an endpoint-appropriate published MVA control where available; do not select
one solely by disease label. Active/inactive SIRT2 comparisons support catalytic
interpretation. Expression-matched tests address intrinsic protein function, while
physiologically constrained expression preserves instability effects. Controls
must account for division defects caused by the perturbations themselves.

## Measurements and statistical unit

The proposed primary endpoint is the fraction of scored completed divisions with
prespecified segregation errors. Define error categories and scoring windows
before unblinding. Record attempted divisions, arrests, death, lost tracks and
unscorable events so that selective exclusion cannot manufacture improvement.
Report these alongside absolute viable-cell output, completed divisions and
checkpoint recovery. Micronuclei alone are an insufficient primary endpoint.

Analyze independent biological preparations/experimental runs, not individual
cells as independent replicates. Randomize conditions within batches; blind
scoring and define exclusions in advance. Estimate candidate-versus-vehicle
effects with confidence intervals using a method that accounts for nesting of
cells within cultures/runs. Examine consistency across independent models and
batches. Separate exploratory concentration selection from confirmatory testing
on fresh biological material. Report every prespecified condition, including nulls.

The laboratory must fix a minimum useful functional improvement, acceptable
output/viability loss and precision requirement before testing the hypothesis.
Choose sample size from baseline variability and the biological unit, not from a
desired P value or arbitrary cell count. No numerical efficacy threshold or
sample size is claimed to be validated here. Prespecify multiplicity handling for
confirmatory comparisons; label secondary/exploratory tests accordingly.

## Mechanistic and exposure gates

1. Measure baseline and post-intervention intracellular NAD and precursor
   utilization. NAPRT abundance is not a substitute for utilization. Without a
   documented baseline deficit, avoid the phrase correction of NAD deficiency.
2. Measure BUBR1 abundance, turnover and localization separately from the functional
   endpoint. A change in bulk cell-cycle composition can alter apparent abundance.
3. Test whether the functional response survives appropriate causal controls.
   SIRT2 dependence alone does not establish exclusive BUBR1 mediation.
4. Use chemically defined nicotinic acid for the initial mechanism test. Select
   concentration/time ranges with laboratory expertise; do not derive them by
   converting an oral dose into a culture concentration. Require an observed
   response curve and exposure justification before translational claims.

## Decision rules

- Invalid model or failed positive control: repair the assay before interpreting
  the candidate; do not hide the failed control.
- No precursor engagement within justified exposure: stop this candidate/model
  branch; no arbitrary escalation or claim against all NAD strategies.
- NAD/BUBR1 increase without better division: molecular response only.
- Reduced error fraction with arrest, death or impaired output: fail the proposed
  functional-rescue criterion, even if a secondary metric improves.
- Functional improvement with preserved output: independently replicate, then
  investigate tissue relevance, longer-term selection and tumor-context safety.
- Benefit without support for the BUBR1 bridge: retain the observation but label
  it an alternative mechanism requiring a new hypothesis, not confirmation.

## Interpretation limits

References 1–4 and 14 motivate these tests; reference 2 supplies mechanistic
controls, not nicotinic-acid rescue. References 15–18 constrain generalization.
Neither short-term viability nor NAPRT staining establishes long-term tumor
safety. Drug-interaction evaluation and specialist oversight precede any clinical
translation. No laboratory commitment, budget or clinical dose is represented.
